MKT 626, Customer-Base Analysis

Why max(PAV) = m(1+d)/d

A customer who never churns still has a finite post-acquisition value. Each monthly payment is discounted one more month than the last, so the payments shrink toward zero and their sum levels off at a ceiling. Churn decides how many customers reach that ceiling; it never moves the ceiling itself.

1. Each payment is worth less than the one before

The payment at month t is worth m/(1+d)t today. The first payment arrives at sign-up (t = 0), so it is not discounted at all. Every later bar is the previous bar divided by (1+d).

2. When does the first m arrive? Two cash-flow timings

The formula depends on when the first payment lands. In our workbook the customer pays at sign-up (t = 0), so the first m is certain and undiscounted. In the Blue Apron case (p. 10), the first margin arrives at the end of month 1 (t = 1), so every payment is discounted one more month. Faint outlines show the nominal m; solid bars show what each payment is worth today.

Paid at sign-up (our workbook)

m + m/(1+d) + m/(1+d)² + … = m(1+d)/d = $1,779.52

The first dollar arrives immediately, so it counts in full.

Paid at the end of month 1 (the case)

m/(1+d) + m/(1+d)² + … = m/d = $1,752.71

The same stream with the first payment removed: exactly one undiscounted m less, or the sign-up total divided by (1+d).

Difference: m(1+d)/d − m/d = m = $26.82, exactly one payment.

3. Stack them up: the running total hits a ceiling

Adding the bars month by month gives the PAV of a customer who survives that long. Each new bar adds less than the last, so the running total keeps rising but can never pass m(1+d)/d. If the first payment were delayed to the end of month 1, the total would level off one payment lower, at m/d.

4. Why the ceiling is exactly m(1+d)/d

Call the never-churn total X. Take away the first payment and what is left is the same never-ending stream, just starting one month later, so it is worth X/(1+d). The picture uses m = $100 and d = 20% so every piece is wide enough to see; the logic is identical for any m and d.

X = m + X/(1+d)the whole stream = first payment + the same stream one month later
X − X/(1+d) = m
X × d/(1+d) = m
X = m(1+d)/d = m + m/dtoday's payment, plus a perpetuity of m starting next month

What churn does, and does not, change

The location of the max

m(1+d)/d = $1,779.52

Set by m and d only. Halving churn, doubling it, or changing a and b leaves it exactly where it is.

The height of the max bar

Pr(PAV = max) = S(t*)

The share of customers who survive long enough that more payments add less than half a cent. Lower churn, or a more polarized cohort with a loyal tail, makes that bar taller and raises E(PAV).
