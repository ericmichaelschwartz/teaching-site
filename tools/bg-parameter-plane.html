What do gamma and delta actually do?

They are not two arbitrary numbers. Together they are the shape of how churn risk is spread across a cohort, and that shape is the reason aggregate retention climbs while nobody changes. Move the point and all three curves follow.

Model and source notes

The model. Each customer has a churn probability that stays constant for life. Across the cohort those probabilities follow Beta(gamma, delta). Aggregate retention is r(t) = (delta + t - 1) / (gamma + delta + t - 1) and survival is the running product, S(t) = S(t-1) x r(t). Equivalently S(t) = B(gamma, delta + t) / B(gamma, delta). Averages: mean churn is gamma / (gamma + delta) and polarization is 1 / (gamma + delta + 1).

Why survivors look loyal. After n renewals the customers still present are not a random sample. Their churn probabilities follow Beta(gamma, delta + n): same gamma, larger delta, so the mix has shifted toward the loyal end. Nobody changed; the churn-prone left first.

Source. Observed survival comes from Class 7 Data - Blue Apron.xlsx, seven months carried into the Blue Apron Data sheet of Class 7 - BG and CLV.xlsx. The fitted values gamma 0.6746182701085135 and delta 1.2336012934958855 are the ones on the Beta Geometric (BG) sheet, found there with Solver by minimising squared error on retention rates for t = 1 to 6. This tool computes the same objective, so the number here matches the workbook.
